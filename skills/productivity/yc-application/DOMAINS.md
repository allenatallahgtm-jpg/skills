# Domain risks

The risks a YC partner expects answered for each kind of company. Answer each one that applies in the Risks section, using the founder's real mitigation; a risk the founder has no answer to yet becomes a placeholder (`[FOUNDER: how you handle clinical liability]`), never an invented mitigation.

## Healthcare

- **Privacy and HIPAA.** Does the product touch protected health information? If so: a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) with every customer and with the cloud provider, where data lives, and the status of any security audit (SOC 2, HITRUST).
- **Clinical liability and FDA.** Does software make or influence a clinical decision? The strong answer keeps a licensed clinician as the decision-maker and logs every decision; if the product is or may be a regulated medical device, say what the regulatory path is.
- **Sales cycle.** Large hospital systems often take a year or more to buy. Name the buyer and the real time from first call to signed contract; a buyer who signs in weeks is a strength worth stating.
- **Who pays, and why.** Patient, provider, payer or employer? Revenue gained (more patients, more reimbursed visits) sells faster than cost saved, so say which one the product delivers.
- **Proof it works.** How fast the outcome shows up, and how it is measured.
- **Integration.** Which medical-records systems the product must connect to, and whether it does today.
- **Reimbursement exposure.** How changes in Medicare, Medicaid or insurer payment rates affect the customer and the product.

## Fintech

- **Licensing.** Money transmission, lending, brokerage or insurance licences needed, and whether the company holds them or works through a licensed partner.
- **Bank or partner dependency.** What happens if the sponsor bank or processor drops the company.
- **Compliance and fraud.** KYC, AML and fraud losses: who carries them and how they are controlled.
- **Credit risk**, for lending: who holds it and how losses are funded.

## B2B and enterprise

- **Sales cycle and buyer.** Who signs, how long it takes, and whether the first deals prove it can repeat.
- **Security review.** SOC 2 status, data handling, single sign-on: what blocks a deal in this market.
- **Incumbent bundling.** Why the large platform the customer already uses won't add this as a feature.

## Consumer

- **Retention.** Week-over-week or month-over-month cohort retention, not sign-ups.
- **Distribution.** How users arrive, and what each one costs to acquire.
- **Novelty.** Whether usage holds once the product stops being new.

## Marketplaces

- **Cold start.** Which side gets built first, and how.
- **Liquidity.** How fast a request gets matched in a launched market.
- **Disintermediation.** Why buyers and sellers keep transacting through the platform after they meet.

## Hardware and deep tech

- **Capital and timeline.** Money and months to a shippable product, and the milestone the next round funds.
- **Technical risk.** What has been proven already (prototype, test results), and what is still unknown.
- **Manufacturing and supply.** Who builds it, at what unit cost, at what volume.

## AI products built on another company's model

- **Platform risk.** What happens if the model provider ships the same feature or changes prices.
- **The moat.** Proprietary data, workflow depth, integrations or distribution that a better model alone doesn't replace.
- **Accuracy and failure.** How errors are caught before they reach the customer.
