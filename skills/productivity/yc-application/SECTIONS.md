# Sections

Section-by-section guidance for the draft, in form order. The YC form's exact wording and fields shift between batches: when the founder pastes their live form, use its wording, field order and character limits, and map the guidance below onto it. Why now, competitors, business model and risks are drafted as their own sections even in a batch where the form folds them into another field: paste each into the field that asks for it, and the founder has them ready for the interview either way.

Write each section as the form question in bold, then the answer beneath it, so the founder can paste answer by answer.

## Company

- **Company name, URL.** As given.
- **What the company does, in 50 characters or less.** The **one-liner**. It says what the product does and for whom, in words a stranger understands in five seconds: "Software that catches freight overcharges." Never the vision ("Reinventing logistics"), never a buzzword stack ("AI-powered platform for..."). Count the characters with a tool where you have one (`printf '%s' "<one-liner>" | wc -m`), and write the count beside the draft so the founder can see it fits.
- **What the company is going to make.** Two or three short paragraphs: the painful status quo for a specific customer, what the product does step by step, then the best proven result. A partner who reads only this answer should be able to explain the company to someone else.
- **Location now and after YC**, and the reason if the form asks.

## Founders

- **Who writes code or does other technical work.** Name each person and what they built. If no founder is technical, say so plainly and say how the product gets built; partners read evasion here as a red flag.
- **Looking for a cofounder?** As given.
- **Something impressive each founder has built or achieved.** One or two sentences per founder, concrete and checkable, with a number where there is one. Prefer things built or won over titles held. Tie at least one to this problem: the founder who did the job by hand is the strongest founder-market fit there is.
- **How the founders know each other.** How long, how they met, and what they have done together. Years of working together answer the cofounder-breakup worry before it is asked.

## Progress

- **How far along.** The stage in one line, then the strongest proof (paying customers, live users, pilots).
- **Time worked, and how much full-time.** Per founder, with dates. A part-time founder states when they go full-time.
- **Tech stack**, including AI models and why they were chosen. One short paragraph; mention compliance setup where the domain demands it.
- **Users and customers.** Active and paying counts, with how "active" is defined. Name customers only with the founder's permission.
- **Revenue.** MRR, growth rate and the window it is measured over, then retention or pilot conversion. If there is no revenue, give the strongest real usage or commitment signal instead (weekly actives, LOIs, waitlist with conversion). Never pad a small number with adjectives: small and growing reads well, vague reads as nothing.
- **Previous applications and accelerators.** What changed since the last application: progress, not just time.

## Idea

- **Why this idea, domain expertise, and how you know people need it.** Lead with the founder's own contact with the problem, then the evidence of need: a number of customer conversations, a pain they paid to solve, conversion from pilots. This is where founder-market fit gets its fullest answer.

## Why now

The specific change, often two or three numbered items: a technology that became good or cheap enough (with a measured result if the founder has one), a regulation, a shift in buyer behaviour or labour market. "AI is getting better" is not a why now; "vision models now read handwritten faxes at 97.8% field accuracy on our test set" is (a fictional example's figure; the founder supplies the real one).

## Competitors

List real competitors and substitutes by category, each with one line on where it stops short. Always include the status quo ("spreadsheets and doing nothing"). Then the **non-obvious insight** in bold: what the founders understand that the others don't, phrased as a claim. "We're faster and easier" is not an insight. Close with why the advantage compounds (data, integrations, network) if the founder's facts support it.

## Business model

How the company makes money (price, unit, average contract value) and how much it could make. Show the market-size math with its inputs (number of customers times annual value) and where each input came from. Bottom-up math from the founder's own pricing beats a top-down "the market is $50B" figure. Name the bigger next step if there is one.

## Risks

The three or four risks a partner in this market would raise, each with the founder's real mitigation. Check [DOMAINS.md](DOMAINS.md) for the domain's expected risks. A risk with no mitigation yet is answered honestly with the plan, never hidden. Also draft **category** and **other ideas considered** here or under Idea, wherever the form puts them; for other ideas, say briefly why this one was chosen first.

## Equity

- **Legal entity**, type and state or country. Flag a non-US or non-Delaware entity as something YC will discuss, without legal advice.
- **Ownership breakdown** in percentages that sum to 100%, and vesting terms.
- **Investment taken**: amount, instrument, cap or valuation, and the type of investor. Exactly as the founder states it.
- **Fundraising now**, as given.

## Curious

- **What convinced you to apply**, and who encouraged it. A specific reason tied to the company beats praise for YC.
- **How you heard about YC.** As given.

## Founder video script

A 1-minute script the founders rehearse from, not read from: about 130 to 150 spoken words. Every founder appears and speaks. In order:

1. Each founder: name and the one credential that matters for this company.
2. The problem, for a specific customer, in one or two sentences.
3. What the product does, in one sentence.
4. The single best number (traction or customer outcome).
5. Why these founders: how long they have known each other, full-time status, who writes code.

Format each line as a quote with the speaker in bold (`> **[A]:** ...`), using the founder's real name or initials. Add one line of direction under the script: one take, no music, no slides or product demo, look at the camera.
